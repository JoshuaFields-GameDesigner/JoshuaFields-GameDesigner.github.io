Combat systems

Decoupling damage and physical response

I designed and tuned two related changes: making impulse more predictable across different masses, then separating damage from physical response so each source could be balanced without destabilising the other.

01 · Problem

Two related problems were obscuring physical feel.

First, identical impulse values produced inconsistent results across differently weighted objects. Later, damage, radial impulse and final ragdoll response were still too tightly coupled. Changes intended to improve one weapon, ability or explosion could alter unrelated behaviour elsewhere.

The design problem was not simply “make reactions stronger”. It was to preserve weapon identity and readable force while keeping lethality and spectacle independently controllable.

02 · Analysis

Separate consistency from source identity.

I traced how configured force passed through mass, velocity limits, falloff, character state and source-specific behaviour. That showed two distinct needs: a predictable baseline across mass, and independent control over the damage and physical response authored for each source.

03 · Solution

Stabilise the baseline, then decouple the channels.

The first change used mass remapping and a maximum-velocity clamp to make equivalent settings behave more consistently. The later AoE work separated physical response from damage, allowing weapons, companion abilities and area effects to retain distinct identities without relying on a single global relationship.

04 · Validation

Iterate across all sources, not one isolated case.

The work went through repeated tuning passes across weapons, grenades, explosions and other damage sources. This included companion-drone grenades, where ability balance and the desired physical response overlapped. Feedback-driven iterations corrected excessive reactions and verified that changes remained predictable across the wider system.

05 · Outcome

Predictable motion without tying spectacle to lethality.

The combined work created a more dependable baseline across mass and gave designers more precise control over physical feel without changing unrelated damage values. It reduced coupling, made regression causes easier to isolate and preserved the intended character of individual weapons and abilities.
