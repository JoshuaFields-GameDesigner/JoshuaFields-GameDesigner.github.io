Grenade Football: from game loop to hardening

I designed and implemented key parts of the mode's connected physical play: launcher feel and football physics, player movement, possession and trajectory cues, and validation of collision and multiplayer dependencies.

01 · Challenge

Physical fun depends on more than physics.

The mode needed satisfying ball interaction, movement suited to physical contests, readable possession, useful trajectory information and stable multiplayer behaviour. A local fix in any one area could easily damage another part of the loop.

02 · Connected design

Make every player-facing system serve the mode.

I treated football physics, launcher behaviour, movement and spatial feedback as parts of the same physical loop. Within that work, I led the redesign of dash and jump for the mode. Dash changed from following camera direction to respecting stick direction, allowing players to tackle directionally without first turning the camera.

03 · Hands-on delivery

Rules, feel and feedback moved together.

The implementation work included repeated football-physics passes, charge and hip-fire behaviour, projectile and impact setup, dash and jump changes, possession markers, landing visuals, nameplates and HUD controls. These were tuned together so movement, ball interaction and feedback supported the same competitive loop.

  • Iterated the core physical interaction across multiple submitted passes.
  • Adapted dash and jump to the needs of the game mode.
  • Improved launcher behaviour around the faster competitive loop.
  • Added clearer spatial and possession feedback.

04 · Multiplayer hardening

Fix the bug without breaking the game mode.

A collision change that addressed a disruptive camera interaction risked interfering with creator-authored goal triggers. I investigated the dependency, tested personal client/server builds and requested targeted QA coverage before committing to the safer solution.

This is the production lesson at the centre of the case: technically correct in isolation is not the same as safe in context.

05 · Outcome

A more coherent and dependable mode.

The work improved the mode's physical core, player movement, competitive readability and presentation while protecting multiplayer and creator-facing dependencies. It demonstrates delivery across connected system design, implementation, UI and risk-led validation.
