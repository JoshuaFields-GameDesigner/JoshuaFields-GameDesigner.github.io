Abilities & AI

Companion drone as a connected player system

I designed the companion as a connected player system, owning its gameplay direction, balancing and iteration across controls, abilities, targeting, resources, progression and feedback.

01 · Problem

A companion only works when its systems agree.

The drone combined direct control, autonomous behaviour, targeting, abilities, energy, cooldowns, progression and HUD feedback. Each part affected the player's understanding of the others, so tuning them separately risked inconsistent rules, unclear availability and surprising outcomes.

02 · System model

Design the relationships, not just the individual abilities.

I mapped the companion as a connected decision system: how the player issued commands, how the drone interpreted targets, what each ability did, how resources governed cadence, how progression changed capability and how UI communicated the current state. This made the wider experience the unit of design rather than a random collection of features.

03 · Design decisions

Make every part reinforce the same player model.

I defined and tuned the relationships between input, selection, response, availability and growth. Targeting profiles considered screen position, range and context; energy, cooldowns, damage and regeneration formed a shared ability economy; progression exposed scalable tuning; and UI requirements evolved alongside behaviour so choices and outcomes were instantly readable by the player.

04 · Iteration

Iterate across the system, not in isolation.

Playtest and review feedback drove repeated passes across controls, damage, regeneration, cooldowns, grenade behaviour, hacking distance, selection profiles, progression values and companion indicators. Each change was evaluated against the wider system so improving one element did not undermine balance, clarity or responsiveness elsewhere.

05 · Outcome

A more coherent and tunable companion experience.

The resulting work aligned controls, targeting, ability behaviour, resources, progression and feedback around a consistent player model. That made individual behaviours easier to understand and tune while preserving the companion as one cohesive system.
