Player guidance

Breadcrumb navigation: from player signal to reusable guidance

I used player feedback and level-specific drop-off signals to identify recurring navigation friction, then designed and implemented persistent guidance for critical routes and scan-triggered help where assistance should remain optional.

01 · Context

Players were losing the route between objectives.

Player feedback and level-specific drop-off signals pointed to recurring navigation friction. Layout, enemy placement, distance and elevation could make the next objective difficult to locate even when the intended route was clear to the development team.

02 · Diagnosis

Not every navigation failure needed the same intervention.

Some locations created critical route-finding problems and required guidance without player input. Elsewhere, many players navigated successfully while a meaningful subset still needed help. Always-on markers in those situations would solve one problem by creating another: unnecessary over-guidance.

03 · Design response

Match the strength of guidance to the severity of the problem.

I designed two related forms of breadcrumbing within one visual language:

  • Persistent guidance for critical locations, activated by gameplay conditions when players needed an unmissable route cue.
  • Scan-triggered guidance as an on-demand safety net where assistance was useful but should not interrupt players already navigating successfully.
The design decision was not simply whether to add a marker. It was when guidance should appear, who should trigger it and how much agency the player should retain.

04 · Delivery

Build a reusable system instead of a collection of level fixes.

I implemented and documented shared Blueprint, AR/UI and data structures covering marker behaviour, triggering, lifetime and display rules. Mission-specific conditions could then use the same underlying model across multiple campaign locations rather than creating unrelated one-off solutions.

05 · Outcome

Significantly lower churn, followed by targeted polish.

The recorded production conclusion was that churn fell significantly after the main rollout. Broad iteration stopped once the result was sufficient; later work focused on specific route corrections, scan reminders, GPS alignment and isolated additions.
